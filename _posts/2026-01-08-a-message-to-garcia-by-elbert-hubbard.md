# A Message to Garcia — And the Message Waiting Inside of You

Twenty years ago, my first real mentor handed me a blank notebook and said, "Figure out how to double our community outreach by Friday." I froze. I asked him who to call, what script to use, what budget I had, and if I could have an extension until Monday. He looked at me with a quiet, devastating sadness and said, "I don't need you to ask me how to build the bridge. I need you to cross the river." That afternoon, I went home and read a twenty-page essay written in 1899 that completely rewired my brain. **The world does not reward those who ask for the map; it rewards those who are willing to forge the trail.**

Let me tell you the story that changed my life. In the spring of 1898, the United States was on the brink of war with Spain, and President McKinley desperately needed to communicate with a Cuban insurgent general named Calixto Garcia. The problem was that no one knew exactly where Garcia was. He was hiding somewhere in the vast, mountainous interior of Cuba, unreachable by mail or telegraph. McKinley turned to his intelligence chief and asked, "Where can I find a man who will carry a message to Garcia?" They brought him a man named Andrew Rowan. McKinley handed Rowan the letter. Rowan did not ask where Garcia was. He did not ask how to get there. He did not ask for a team, a guide, or a guarantee of safety. He simply took the letter, sealed it in an oilskin pouch, strapped it over his heart, and went to Cuba. **Greatness is never found in the absence of obstacles; it is found in the refusal to let obstacles dictate your destination.**

Elbert Hubbard wrote that story down in a single hour, and it became one of the most reprinted essays in human history. Historians will quickly point out that Hubbard took massive creative liberties with the facts of Rowan’s actual military mission. But as a mentor, I am not interested in the historical record; I am interested in the parable. Hubbard captured a profound, undeniable truth about the architecture of the human soul. This essay is not really about being a good employee, and it is certainly not about blind obedience to a boss. It is about what happens when you are handed the raw materials of your life and asked to do something with them. **You are not an employee of your circumstances; you are the chief executive of your own potential.**

When I look at the people I mentor today, the biggest thief of their destiny is not a lack of talent. It is the illusion of readiness. We have been conditioned to believe that we need more instructions before we can take action. We wait for the perfect business plan, the perfect time, the perfect amount of money in the bank. We ask a hundred questions to stall the moment of truth. But the harvest does not wait for the farmer to feel ready; it waits for the farmer to plant. Rowan did not wait for a GPS or a paved road. He navigated by the stars and his own relentless grit. **Preparation is often just the procrastination of the fearful, dressed up as wisdom.**

So what does "taking the message" actually look like in your Tuesday morning? It looks like waking up at 5:30 a.m. to write your book while the house is still quiet, instead of waiting for an agent to discover you. It looks like picking up the phone and making the ten cold calls you have been avoiding, instead of asking your manager for a better lead list. It looks like looking at your health, taking full ownership of the mirror, and choosing the walk over the couch without needing a cheerleader. **Discipline is simply the act of making a promise to your future self and refusing to break it when the present moment gets uncomfortable.**

I know there is a voice in your head right now telling you that you are not qualified, that your "Garcia" is too far away, that the mountains of your current reality are too steep to climb. I need you to silence that voice. The Creator of the universe did not breathe life into your lungs so you could spend your days asking for permission to use them. He put a specific, unrepeatable message inside of you—your art, your business, your kindness, your leadership—and He is trusting you to deliver it to the world. **Your setback is not a stop sign; it is simply a detour designed to build the muscle you will need for your next level.**

What if you fail? What if you take the letter and get lost in the jungle? Here is the truth I have learned after watching hundreds of people try and stumble: a mistake made in motion is infinitely more valuable than a perfect plan trapped in your head. When you move, you gather data. When you move, you correct your course. When you move, you signal to the universe that you are a participant in your own life, not a spectator. The soil of your life is fertile, but it requires the friction of your footsteps to yield a crop. **You cannot steer a parked car, and you cannot compound the growth of a seed you refuse to put in the ground.**

You are closer to your breakthrough than you realize. The only thing standing between you and the life you are dreaming of is a single decision to stop asking "how" and start walking "forward." So here is what I want you to do today. Identify your Garcia. What is the one project, the one conversation, the one habit you have been waiting for someone else to hand you a manual for? Write it down on a piece of paper. And then, before the sun goes down, take one undeniable step toward it. Send the draft. Buy the domain name. Put on your running shoes. You do not need the whole map. You just need to take the letter. **Your life changes the exact second you decide to stop waiting for a map and start walking.** Now go deliver your message.
