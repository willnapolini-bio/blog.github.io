# The Life Power and How to Use It by Elizabeth Towne: How to Stop Leaking Your Energy and Start Living

Have you ever noticed how some people can work a fourteen-hour day and still have the energy to laugh over dinner, while others work six hours and collapse into the couch, utterly depleted?

We usually attribute this to genetics, or age, or caffeine intake. But after years of studying human endurance and watching people burn out, I have come to a different conclusion. 

The difference is rarely about how much energy you generate. It is about how much energy you leak.

In 1906, a pioneering writer and editor named Elizabeth Towne published a slender, quietly revolutionary book called *The Life Power and How to Use It*. Towne was a central figure in the New Thought movement, and she understood something that our modern hustle culture has entirely forgotten: vitality is not something you have to manufacture through sheer willpower. It is something you already possess, and your only job is to stop blocking it.

Towne’s central thesis is deceptively simple. There is an infinite "Life Power"—a current of physical, mental, and spiritual vitality—that flows through you constantly. You do not need to beg for it, hustle for it, or earn it. You simply need to get out of its way.

What I want to show you today is how Towne’s century-old insights map perfectly onto our modern epidemic of exhaustion, and how you can use her principles to plug the leaks in your own life.

## The Crimped Hose: A Story of Blocked Potential

Imagine a garden hose connected to a powerful municipal water main. The water pressure is immense. The supply is practically infinite. But somewhere along the length of the hose, there is a sharp kink. And halfway down, someone has placed a heavy cinderblock on the rubber.

If you stand at the nozzle and wonder why only a pathetic trickle is coming out, what do you do? 

Do you go back to the water main and scream at it to push harder? Do you try to invent a new kind of water? No. You walk the length of the hose, find the kink, remove the cinderblock, and let the water do what it was already designed to do.

Towne’s "Life Power" is the water main. Your body and mind are the hose. And the kinks? They are your worries, your chronic physical tension, your fear of the future, and your habit of mentally rehearsing failure.

When you feel depleted, the answer is rarely to push harder. The answer is to find the kink and remove it.

## Worry Is the Ultimate Energy Leak

Towne observed something that modern neuroscience is only now fully quantifying: worry is perhaps the most fruitless expenditure of life power imaginable.

When you worry, you are essentially running your mental engine in neutral while slamming on the gas. You are burning through vast reserves of glucose, emotional bandwidth, and physical stamina to solve problems that have not even happened yet. 

I have sat across from countless brilliant, capable people who were entirely paralyzed by their own lives. They were not lacking in skill. They were lacking in current. They had drained their batteries overnight, lying awake at 2 a.m., rehearsing a difficult conversation with a colleague or agonizing over a financial projection.

Towne’s remedy was not to tell people to "just stop worrying"—which is as useless as telling a drowning person to just stop sinking. Instead, she taught the practice of *mental substitution*. You cannot empty the mind of a worry by fighting it; you can only crowd it out by deliberately focusing on a constructive, positive vision.

Your energy flows exactly where your attention goes. If you stare at the locked door, you will never see the open window.

## The Architecture of Relaxation

We live in a culture that treats relaxation as a reward for exhaustion. We work until we drop, and then we "collapse" into leisure. But Towne argued that relaxation is not the reward for work; it is the prerequisite for power.

In her writing, she frequently emphasized the physical mechanics of the body, specifically the solar plexus—the complex network of nerves situated behind the stomach that she viewed as the physical battery of the human system. She noted that when we are afraid, anxious, or trying to force an outcome, we physically tense our abdominal muscles and restrict our breathing. We literally pinch off our own power supply.

To use the Life Power, you must first yield.

I know this sounds counterintuitive to the modern ear. We are taught that achievement requires tension, grit, and grinding effort. But watch a master athlete, a brilliant musician, or a truly effective leader in their element. They are rarely rigid. They possess a fluid, relaxed readiness. They are not forcing the action; they are allowing it to happen through them.

Physical relaxation is not laziness. It is the removal of internal friction. When you unclench your jaw, drop your shoulders, and breathe deeply into your belly, you are not giving up. You are unclogging the pipe.

## Vision Before Effort: Seeing It Done

One of the most famous maxims to emerge from Towne’s work is the instruction to "see the things you want as yours already."

This is often misunderstood as magical thinking—the idea that if you just sit on your couch and visualize a new car, it will appear in your driveway. That is not what Towne meant, and it is not how life works.

What she understood was the psychology of identity and action. If you operate from a mindset of *lacking*, your actions will be frantic, desperate, and short-sighted. You will take bad deals, tolerate poor treatment, and burn out trying to fill a void.

But if you cultivate the internal state of already being resourced, capable, and secure, your actions change entirely. You negotiate better. You speak with quiet authority. You make decisions from a place of abundance rather than scarcity.

You do not visualize the outcome to magically conjure it. You visualize the outcome to rewire your own nervous system so that you can do the work required to build it. 

You cannot build a confident life on a foundation of desperate thoughts.

## The Steering Wheel: The Power of the "I Am"

Towne placed immense weight on the words "I am." She viewed them as the steering wheel for your Life Power. Whatever you attach to those two words, your subconscious mind and your physical body will marshal their resources to prove you right.

If you wake up and say, "I am so tired," "I am so stressed," "I am terrible with money," you are issuing a direct command to your nervous system. Your body will dutifully slump, your cortisol will spike, and your brain will narrow its focus to confirm your limitation.

But if you practice saying, "I am capable," "I am grounded," "I am learning," you redirect the current.

This is not about lying to yourself. If you are deeply in debt, standing in front of a mirror and screaming "I am a millionaire" will only create cognitive dissonance and make you feel foolish. But you can truthfully say, "I am resourceful. I am taking responsibility. I am building a better system."

Character precedes circumstances. By claiming the character trait you wish to embody, you generate the energy needed to alter your circumstances.

## Why This Matters in the Modern World

You might be wondering why a book from 1906 matters in an era of smartphones, artificial intelligence, and global supply chain crises.

It matters because human biology has not changed. Your nervous system does not know the difference between a physical predator chasing you through the woods and the glowing red notification badge on your email app. Both trigger the same tension, the same shallow breathing, the same kinking of the hose.

We are living through an epidemic of chronic fatigue, not because we are doing too much physical labor, but because we are carrying too much unexpressed mental tension. We are leaking our Life Power into the ether of the internet, into arguments we are having in the shower, into the dread of Monday morning.

If you can learn to plug these leaks, you will not just get more done. You will enjoy the time you are alive. You will have the energy to play with your children, to pursue a creative hobby, to sit quietly without reaching for a screen to numb the exhaustion.

You do not need more hours in the day. You need more life in your hours.

## Practical Application

Understanding the Life Power is a philosophy. Applying it is a practice. Here is how you can begin unclogging your hose this week.

### 1. The Daily Tension Audit
Set an alarm on your phone for three random times during your workday. When it goes off, do not check your email. Instead, do a rapid scan of your body. Are your shoulders hiked up to your ears? Is your jaw clenched? Are you holding your breath while you read this screen?

Take ten seconds to consciously drop your shoulders, unclench your teeth, and take one deep breath into your belly. You will be amazed at how much energy you reclaim simply by realizing you were holding it hostage.

### 2. The Worry Containment Strategy
You cannot eliminate worry entirely, but you can stop it from draining you all day. When a worry arises, write it down on a physical piece of paper. Tell yourself, "I have captured this thought. It is safe on the paper. I do not need to carry it in my head right now."

Then, schedule a "worry window"—say, 4:30 p.m. to 4:45 p.m. If the worry pops up at 10 a.m., remind yourself that you will deal with it at 4:30. Often, by the time 4:30 arrives, the emotional charge has dissipated, and you can look at the problem with clear, rational eyes.

### 3. Rewrite Your "I Am"
Pay attention to your casual self-talk for one week. Notice how often you use "I am" to describe a negative state. "I'm such an idiot." "I'm exhausted." "I'm overwhelmed."

Catch yourself. Pause. And consciously rephrase it to describe a constructive action or identity. "I am learning." "I am resting." "I am navigating a heavy workload." 

It will feel awkward at first. Do it anyway. You are reprogramming the steering wheel.

## Who Should Read This Book

*The Life Power and How to Use It* is a vital read for anyone who feels chronically depleted despite getting eight hours of sleep. It is for the entrepreneur, the parent, the creative, and the corporate worker who feels like they are pushing a boulder up a hill every single day. If you are interested in the historical roots of mindset work, energy management, and the New Thought movement that paved the way for modern personal development, Towne is a foundational voice.

It may be less useful for you if you are looking for highly technical, clinical advice on neurology, or if you prefer strictly secular, data-driven productivity systems. Towne writes with a spiritual conviction that was characteristic of her era. You do not have to share her specific metaphysical beliefs to benefit from her profound psychological insights, but you do need to be open to the idea that your internal state dictates your external reality.

## One Small Step

I want you to try something tonight, right before you go to sleep.

When you turn off the lamp, do not let your mind drift into the anxieties of tomorrow. Instead, lie flat on your back. Let your arms rest loosely at your sides. Consciously relax your toes, your calves, your thighs, your stomach, your chest, your hands, your face. Feel the mattress supporting you.

And as you breathe, silently repeat one simple phrase: *I am at peace. I am recharging. The Life Power is flowing through me.*

Do not try to force sleep. Just practice the relaxation. Let the kinks out of the hose. 

You have spent enough time trying to force the water out of a crimped hose. Stop pushing. Start releasing. The power you are looking for is already inside you, waiting for you to simply get out of its way. 

The current is waiting. Open the valve.
