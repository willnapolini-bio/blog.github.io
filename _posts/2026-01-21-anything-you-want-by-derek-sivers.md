# Anything You Want — And Why Your Work Should Be Your Utopia

What if the reason you feel exhausted by your work is not because you are doing too much, but because you are building someone else’s universe instead of your own? I want to tell you something I had to learn the hard way: you do not have to play by the rules of a game you never agreed to play. For years, I thought success meant scaling up, grinding harder, and conforming to a rigid blueprint of what a "real" business or a "real" career looked like. I was chasing a mirage. Then I read Derek Sivers’ *Anything You Want*, and it felt like someone opened a window in a stuffy room. Sivers spent ten years building CD Baby, eventually selling it for $22 million, but the true wealth he accumulated was not in his bank account—it was in his realization that when you create something, you get to make a little universe where you create all the laws. **You are not just building a business or a career; you are designing your utopia.**

## The Origin: A Musician and a CD

Sivers did not start with a grand vision or a fifty-page business plan. He was a professional musician who just wanted to sell his own CD on his personal website. Friends saw his site and asked if he could sell theirs, too. He said yes. He did not have venture capital. He did not have a master plan. He just started answering calls for help. And in doing so, he built one of the most beloved independent music stores on the internet. **Your grand purpose rarely announces itself with trumpets; it usually starts as a quiet favor to a friend.**

## Principle 1: Build Your Utopia

Most people start a project and immediately ask, "What does the market want?" or "What will make the most money?" Sivers flips this entirely. He argues that your business, your side hustle, and your daily work should be a reflection of your ideal world. If you hate long meetings, ban them. If you love handwritten notes, make them your standard. You are the architect. **Never shrink yourself to fit into a system you have the power to redesign.**

## Principle 2: The Multiplier of Execution

We live in a world that worships the "big idea." People guard their ideas with non-disclosure agreements, terrified someone will steal their million-dollar concept. Sivers cuts through this noise with a principle that will save you years of frustration: *Ideas are just a multiplier of execution*. An idea is worth maybe twenty dollars. Execution is worth millions. **A mediocre idea executed with relentless, daily discipline will always defeat a brilliant idea trapped in a notebook.** Stop waiting for the perfect concept. Start laying bricks.

## Principle 3: The "Hell Yeah!" Filter

As you grow, opportunities will come. People will ask for your time, your partnership, your energy. The default human response is to say yes out of guilt or fear of missing out. Sivers gives us a razor-sharp framework: If an opportunity does not make you say, "HELL YEAH!", then your answer is no. This is not about being arrogant; it is about protecting your soil. If you plant every seed handed to you, your garden will choke on weeds. **Your greatest asset is not your talent; it is your ruthless protection of your focus.**

## Principle 4: Thrill Your People

Here is the secret that separates the forgotten from the legendary: care about your people more than you care about yourself. Sivers believes that business is not about money; it is about making dreams come true for others. When you focus entirely on thrilling your current customers—even if it occasionally costs you a little money in the short term—they become your greatest marketing team. He famously wrote the most charming, human order confirmation emails in the history of e-commerce, and it built an empire of loyalty. **People do not remember your scale; they remember how you made them feel when they needed you.**

## The Cost of Losing Your Way

Sivers is honest about his failures. He tells the story of a time he lost $3.3 million because he tried to automate a process just to save ten minutes. He stripped the humanity out of his business for the sake of efficiency, and it cost him dearly. I share this with you because you will be tempted to optimize the soul out of your work. You will be tempted to scale before you are ready, to outsource the very thing that makes you special. **Efficiency is a tool, but humanity is the foundation.** Do not trade your magic for a marginal gain in speed.

## Your One Small Step

So here is what I want you to do today. Not next quarter. Today. Look at your calendar or your to-do list for tomorrow. Find one thing that drains you, one obligation that you only do because you think you "should." Cancel it. Delegate it. Automate it. Or simply say no. Then, take that reclaimed hour and pour it into thrilling just one single person you serve. Send a handwritten note. Make a five-minute phone call just to check in. Build a tiny piece of your utopia. The universe you want is waiting for you to start building it. Go make your first brick.
